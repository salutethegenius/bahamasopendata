The Strategic Position

BahamasOpenData is not a government agency and not a tech startup. It lives in between — civic infrastructure with the accessibility of a public utility. The mark must carry institutional weight without looking bureaucratic.

The One Ownable Thing

The Bahamas is an archipelago of 700 islands. A data platform built specifically for this geography can use that geography as its symbol. Nodes + connections = islands + data relationships. No other open data platform in the world can make this mark.

The Typography Logic

Sora — rounded, geometric, highly legible at all sizes. Not a legacy serif, not a startup grotesque. A font that reads as infrastructure you can trust. Paired with DM Mono for data labels, dataset metadata, API references, and all technical surfaces.

The Archipelago Network

The node positions are not random — they approximate the geographic scatter of the major Bahamian islands from northwest to southeast. Nassau (New Providence) is the largest node with a halo ring, marking it as the data hub and capital simultaneously.

Teal connections represent active, rich data relationships. Ocean-navy connections represent passive or supporting links. The density of the eastern chain reads as data density — more connections = richer coverage.

  • Nassau node: largest, with connection halo ring
  • Teal connections: primary data network
  • Navy connections: secondary/supporting
  • "Bahamas" — Sora 700, ocean, dominant
  • "Open" — Sora 300 italic weight, teal
  • "Data" — Sora 600, ocean — the noun it delivers
  • Domain line: DM Mono, only at ≥240px width

The Open Grid

A data table with its top row broken free — teal, extended, escaping the closed grid. The lower rows remain locked in ocean navy, representing data still in government silos. The top row is what BahamasOpenData does: takes it out.

The open ring at the terminus of the teal line is the aperture motif — the data is accessible now, the gap is the openness itself.

  • Grid: 4 columns × 4 rows, uniform spacing
  • Top row: teal, extended 16px beyond grid boundary
  • Open ring: 3px stroke circle + filled center dot
  • Lower grid: 40% opacity navy — closed/siloed state
  • Works at favicon scale — grid reduces to 3×3

The Aperture

A circle that doesn't close — the gap is the openness, rendered as geometry. The two teal endpoint dots mark where the ring opens, connected by a dashed line that implies the data channel that's been created. Data points cluster inside.

The cleanest mark of the three — easiest to render at 16px, strongest as an app icon. Weakest at telling the Bahamian geography story. Best used as the icon/favicon complement to the Archipelago primary.

  • Outer arc: 300°, leaves 60° gap at top-right
  • Gap endpoints: teal filled circles, equal size
  • Dashed connector: 3px dash, 3px gap, 40% opacity
  • Interior dots: teal scatter — the data inside the system
  • Inner ring: 20% opacity — depth only
Typography System
Wordmark Construction
Bahamas

Sora 700. Ocean. The geography first — the country this data belongs to. Heavy, authoritative, never italic. It's the noun that owns everything that follows.

Open

Sora 300. Teal. The thinnest weight in the family — openness communicated through lightness itself. The word is the value, the weight is the expression of it. Never bold. Never navy.

Data

Sora 600–700. Ocean. The product. Returns to weight after the openness of "Open" — the data is real, structured, reliable. The weight contrast between "Open" and "Data" is intentional tension.

Colour logic

Ocean deep is the Bahamian sea at night — the deep-water authority of a serious data infrastructure. It grounds every surface that needs weight: headers, marks, body text, authority states.

Bahamian teal is the shallow-water turquoise that is literally the identifying colour of these islands from satellite. It signals openness, accessibility, and the specific geography this platform exists to serve. No other data platform has this colour as a legitimate claim.
